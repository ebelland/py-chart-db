P1 - HIGH LEVERAGE: do these before piling on features
================================================================

P1-1  series operations: declarative parameter spec  <-- DO THIS FIRST
      The eight operations run 775-1705 lines each, ~10,000 lines total,
      against a 964-line base. Most of it is form building, signal wiring
      and state persistence repeated eight times. Let an operation declare
      its parameters as data and have the base build the UI:

          PARAMS = (
              FloatParam("window", label="Window", default=11,
                         range=(3, 501), odd_only=True),
              ChoiceParam("mode", label="Method", choices=(...)),
          )

      Discovery is already drop-in (series_operation_scanner AST-scans for
      SeriesOperationDialogBase subclasses and reads Name/Description/Icon),
      so this is the missing half. Every item in P1-3 and P2-2 gets cheaper
      afterwards - which is why it goes first.

P1-2  ChartPanel: hover readout
      No motion_notify_event is connected anywhere; the chart is inert until
      clicked. Biggest perceived-quality win. Two things that matter: throttle
      it, and blit rather than full-draw or it will feel terrible on large
      series.

P1-3  series operation: Calculus (derivative + integral)
      Confirmed absent - no np.gradient, no scipy.integrate, no find_peaks
      anywhere in the codebase.
        derivative: np.gradient (handles non-uniform x), Savitzky-Golay with
          deriv=1,2 (savgol_filter is already imported in the smoothing
          dialog), UnivariateSpline.derivative()
        integral: cumulative_trapezoid for a running integral, simpson/
          trapezoid for a definite value
      Noise amplification is the whole difficulty of numerical differentiation,
      so smoothing belongs inside this operation, not as a step the user is
      expected to remember first. Baseline subtraction likewise belongs inside
      the integral, or peak areas are meaningless.

P1-4  series operation: peak finding
      scipy.signal.find_peaks + peak_prominences + peak_widths. Very little
      code, large payoff, and it pairs directly with P1-3: find a peak, then
      integrate between its bounds. Given a calibration table and a spectral
      dialog, this may be worth more than the derivative.

P1-5  ChartPanel: bars and wedges are unpickable, so clicking them does nothing
      chart_panel_widget.py:385 arms only axes.lines and axes.collections. The
      docstring's reasoning is sound - a patch has no per-point index, so it
      would report "1 point" and mean the whole shape - but the consequence is
      that bar and pie charts are entirely inert. Fix by giving patches their
      own readout reporting category and value, not by arming them into the
      existing point path.

P1-6  ChartPanel: legend picking to toggle series visibility
      Standard Matplotlib recipe, roughly 20 lines, constantly wanted on
      multi-series plots.
