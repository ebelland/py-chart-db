N-18  style: corner-rounding artifact on nested rounded panels (e.g. series
      operations dialogs)
      Screenshot showed a small wedge of the plain white background showing
      through where two rounded-corner cards meet (an inner corner, not an
      outer one) - incomplete clipping rather than a missing radius. Needs
      reproducing live (which series-operation dialog, which corner) before
      a fix: likely a child widget painting square behind a parent's
      rounded QSS border-radius, or two adjacent cards' curves not meeting
      cleanly at a T-junction.

N-19  GUI: toward a more standard, easy, intuitive interface
      Requested generically ("una GUI piu' standard, facile ed intuitiva");
      these are the concrete points actually observed hands-on this round,
      not a generic UX checklist - each names a specific place, not a
      principle.
      - Properties panels (Figure/Axis/Series) are Apply-then-look: nothing
        shows what a change will do before it is applied and the chart
        redraws. Even a lightweight inline preview (a small thumbnail, or
        highlighting which control touches which part of the chart) would
        close that loop faster than switching eyes between the form and
        the tab.
      - The Series properties selector is a plain-text combo; each entry's
        series already has an actual plotted colour. A small colour swatch
        per row (matching MatplotlibColorCombo's own approach elsewhere)
        would make "which series is this" recognisable without opening it.
      - The activity rail (Data / Chart options / Series operations) is
        icon-only with tooltip-only labels - fine once learned, but nothing
        in the running app teaches a first-time user what the four icons
        are for beyond hovering each one. Worth checking whether a
        one-time label (or a "?" walkthrough) is worth the added chrome,
        given nav rail structure itself is being kept as-is per the
        Finder-sidebar discussion.
      - Import dialog shows delimiter/encoding/skip-rows controls
        regardless of the chosen source - irrelevant for Excel or a
        database table, relevant only for delimited text. Progressive
        disclosure (hide what the current source cannot use) would read as
        less cluttered than always-visible-but-inert controls.
      - Manual spacing (Figure properties) asks for six raw 0..1 fractions
        with no visual feedback - a live-updating preview, or drag handles
        on the chart itself, is the more standard way this kind of control
        is usually presented, though it is a real redesign, not a quick
        pass.
