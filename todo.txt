P1-1  [DONE] series operations: declarative parameter spec
      The eight operations run 775-1705 lines each, ~10,000 lines total,
      against a 964-line base. Most of it is form building, signal wiring
      and state persistence repeated eight times. Let an operation declare
      its parameters as data and have the base build the UI:

          PARAMS = (
              FloatParam("window", label="Window", default=11,
                         range=(3, 501), odd_only=True),
              ChoiceParam("mode", label="Method", choices=(...)),
          )

      Built as parameter_spec.py (plain data, no Qt) + parameter_form.py
      (the widgets). Outlier converted as proof.
      CORRECTION to the estimate above: it did NOT collapse the file. Outlier
      went 790 -> 794 lines. For a three-parameter dialog a declaration with
      tooltips and visibility rules costs about what the imperative version
      cost. The gain is in the invariants - visibility rules and widgets are
      the same data so they cannot drift, a parameter cannot be added without
      its signal connection, values() always returns every declared name, and
      odd-window rounding happens in one place. Judge the remaining seven
      conversions on that, not on line count.

P1-2  [DONE] ChartPanel: hover readout
      Throttled to 40ms and blitted. Measured 8x5in @100dpi, mean of 25:
        1k x1   full 12.03ms  blit 2.54ms + hit 0.03ms  = 4.7x
        100k x1 full 14.50ms  blit 2.55ms + hit 0.91ms  = 4.2x
        50k x10 full 26.33ms  blit 2.58ms + hit 4.63ms  = 3.7x
      The blit is flat at ~2.55ms whatever the data size. What grows is the
      hit test, and at 500k points it is already the larger half - which is
      the argument for P2-5, not for a bigger throttle.

P1-3  [DONE] series operation: Calculus (derivative + integral)
      app/series_operations/series_calculus_dialog.py. Verified against
      analytic results: d/dx sin = cos to 1.7e-07 (spline), integral of sin
      over [0,pi] = 2.0000000007 (Simpson).
      Both design claims measured, not assumed:
       - Savitzky-Golay beats raw np.gradient by ~8x RMS on noisy data, which
         is why smoothing is inside the derivative rather than a step before.
       - A gaussian peak (true area 1.7725) on an offset of 5 integrates to
         51.77 without baseline subtraction. 97% of the answer was baseline.
        derivative: np.gradient (handles non-uniform x), Savitzky-Golay with
          deriv=1,2 (savgol_filter is already imported in the smoothing
          dialog), UnivariateSpline.derivative()
        integral: cumulative_trapezoid for a running integral, simpson/
          trapezoid for a definite value
      Noise amplification is the whole difficulty of numerical differentiation,
      so smoothing belongs inside this operation, not as a step the user is
      expected to remember first. Baseline subtraction likewise belongs inside
      the integral, or peak areas are meaningless.

P1-4  [DONE] series operation: peak finding
      app/series_operations/series_peaks_dialog.py. Reports each peak with
      its prominence, width at half prominence, and the x bounds of that
      width - which are what P1-3's integral wants.
      Prominence is the default filter, not height, and the test data shows
      why: on three gaussians over a sloping baseline, prominence finds
      exactly the 3 true peaks while height returns 50 spurious ones, because
      on a rising baseline everything late in the series is "high".

P1-5  [DONE] ChartPanel: bars and wedges are unpickable
      chart_panel_widget.py:385 arms only axes.lines and axes.collections. The
      docstring's reasoning is sound - a patch has no per-point index, so it
      would report "1 point" and mean the whole shape - but the consequence is
      that bar and pie charts are entirely inert. Fix by giving patches their
      own readout reporting category and value, not by arming them into the
      existing point path.
      Done via _describe_patch. Bar orientation is read from the
      BarContainer, not guessed from geometry: a tall thin barh bar and a
      tall thin bar bar are the same rectangle, and the first attempt at
      guessing got barh wrong.

P1-6  [DONE] ChartPanel: legend picking to toggle series visibility
      Handles matched to artists by label, not by position, so a series drawn
      as several artists (a line plus its error bars) toggles as one. Hidden
      series are skipped by the P1-2 hover hit test.
